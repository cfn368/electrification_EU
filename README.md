# Elektrificering i EU — Shift-share dekomponering af elektrificeringsrater

Developed by Linus Lindquist for [Erhvervslivets Tænketank](https://www.etank.dk).

Analysis quantifying how much of the difference in electrification rates across EU countries — and over time — is driven by sector-specific improvements in electrification intensity versus changes in the sectoral composition of energy consumption. The decomposition applies a symmetric shift-share identity with midpoint weights, which eliminates the interaction term and yields an exact accounting.

## Data sources

| Source | Content | Access |

| [Eurostat `nrg_bal_c`](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/nrg_bal_c) | Total energy consumption (TJ) by sector — decomposition weights | Live API via SDMX 2.1 (`fetch_energy_weights()`) |

Active countries: **DK, SE, DE, NL, FR, FI, EU27_2020**. UK excluded (poor data quality); ES excluded (not available in energy balance dataset).

Processed data is cached in `0_intermediate/` as parquet files so downstream notebooks run without re-fetching.

## Key analysis steps

### 1. Data fetch and construction (`1_fetch`)

Electrification rates (%) are fetched from `nrg_ind_re` at three aggregation levels: total final consumption, three parent sectors (industry, transport, other), and all subsectors. Total energy consumption (TJ) is fetched from `nrg_bal_c` with `SIEC=TOTAL` and serves as the decomposition weight.

Four subsectors are excluded: `FC_TRA_DAVI_E` (domestic aviation), `FC_TRA_DNAVI_E` (domestic navigation), `FC_TRA_NSP_E`, and `FC_OTH_NSP_E` — all lack meaningful energy weight data. A coverage check confirms that included sectors account for ≥97% of total final consumption for all countries.

### 2. Descriptive analysis (`2_electrification_rates`, `2_total_use`)

Time-series plots of the aggregate electrification rate and the three parent sectors for all countries since 1995. A 4-column subsector grid shows cross-country differences at the most granular available level. A parallel notebook plots each sector's share of total final energy consumption, with a sanity check confirming shares sum to ~100% per country-year.

### 3. Shift-share decomposition (`3_decomposition`)

The electrification rate for country $c$ in year $t$ is the energy-weighted average of sector rates:

$$E_{c,t} = \sum_i s_{i,c,t} \cdot e_{i,c,t}$$

where $s_{i,c,t}$ is sector $i$'s share of total final energy consumption (TJ) and $e_{i,c,t}$ is its electrification rate. The change decomposes exactly — without a residual — using midpoint weights:

$$\Delta E = \underbrace{\sum_i \Delta s_i \cdot \bar{e}_i}_{\text{composition}} + \underbrace{\sum_i \bar{s}_i \cdot \Delta e_i}_{\text{intensity}}, \qquad \bar{s}_i = \tfrac{s_0 + s_1}{2},\; \bar{e}_i = \tfrac{e_0 + e_1}{2}$$

Two analyses:
- **Time series** — first to last observation per country: how much of each country's gain is explained by intensity improvements vs. sectoral rebalancing?
- **Cross-section** — each country vs. EU-27 in the latest common year: what explains DK's gap below the EU average?

### 4. Green energy shares (`4_greenpower`)

Tracks the share of green energy in gross inland consumption (GIC) and gross electricity production (GEP) since 1995. Sources: hydro (`RA100`), wind (`RA300`), solar PV (`RA420`), nuclear (`N900H`), solid biomass and renewable waste (`R5110-5150_W6000RI`). Shown as time-series lines and stacked source-contribution bars for the latest year, separately for GIC and GEP.

### 5. Road transport case study (`5_case_study`)

A counterfactual: if `FC_TRA_ROAD_E` (all road transport — trucks, cars, buses, motorcycles) were electrified to 100%, by how much would the EU-27 aggregate electrification rate improve?

$$\Delta E_\text{agg} = s_\text{road} \cdot (1 - e_\text{road}/100)$$

where $s_\text{road}$ is road transport's share of total final energy consumption (from `df_enw.parquet`) and $e_\text{road}$ is its current electrification rate. Note: `FC_TRA_ROAD_E` cannot be disaggregated into passenger vs. freight within `nrg_bal_c`; doing so requires IEA Extended Energy Balances or ODYSSEE-MURE.

## How to run

No `requirements.txt` is included; the analysis uses standard scientific Python. Install with:

```bash
pip install numpy pandas matplotlib scipy statsmodels requests pyarrow
```

All imports and fetch functions are centralised in `pylib/preamble.py`. Each notebook opens with:

```python
from pylib.preamble import *
enable_autoreload()
```

Run notebooks in order:

1. `1_fetch` — fetches from Eurostat and writes all parquets to `0_intermediate/`. Requires internet.
2. `2_electrification_rates` and `2_total_use` — read from parquet; independent of each other.
3. `3_decomposition` — reads `df.parquet`.
4. `4_greenpower` — fetches directly from Eurostat on every run. Requires internet.
5. `5_case_study` — reads parquet and fetches energy weights from Eurostat. Requires internet.

Delete a parquet file in `0_intermediate/` to force a re-fetch of that dataset.
